# Analysis plan: sex differences in drug-induced liver injury reports (FAERS)

Status: version 1, fixed on 2026-11-23 BEFORE any raw FAERS file was downloaded or tabulated by sex.
Only data seen before this plan: four openFDA count queries (report counts by sex for the preferred terms Drug-induced liver injury, Hepatocellular injury and Mixed liver injury). They showed that the terms exist in adequate numbers. They informed no threshold below.
Any later change is logged as a dated amendment at the bottom with its reason and whether outcome data had been seen.

## 1. Question
Do women and men differ in (a) how often hepatic injury is reported for the same drug, (b) the reported injury phenotype, and (c) fatal outcome of reported hepatic injury, once the comparison is made within the same drug instead of across different drug mixes.

## 2. Data
- Source: FDA Adverse Event Reporting System quarterly ASCII files, 2014Q3 to the latest quarter published at download time. Start quarter chosen because the active-ingredient field prod_ai begins there.
- Files used: DEMO, DRUG, REAC, OUTC, INDI. Deleted-case lists applied where FDA publishes them.
- Deduplication, primary: one record per caseid, the one with the latest fda_dt, ties broken by highest primaryid.
- Deduplication, sensitivity: also collapse records that match on sex, age, event date, reporter country, the sorted set of suspect ingredients and the sorted set of preferred terms.
- A manifest with file name, byte size and SHA-256 of every downloaded zip is kept.

## 3. Populations
- Sex set: deduplicated reports with sex coded F or M.
- Adult set: Sex set with age known and 18 years or older. Age converted to years from age and age_cod. Ages above 120 set to missing.
- Primary analyses exclude reports carrying any overdose, poisoning, self-harm, misuse or abuse preferred term (list in section 5). Registries of idiosyncratic DILI exclude such cases. Sensitivity analysis includes them.

## 4. Exposure
- Drug: normalized active ingredient from prod_ai, role codes PS and SS only. Combination products are split on the backslash separator and each ingredient is counted. Salt and ester words are stripped by a fixed word list. A manual merge table for the ingredients that pass the count thresholds is reviewed by name only, without sex-specific counts.
- Stratum variable for within-drug analyses: the primary suspect (PS) ingredient string after normalization (a combination product keeps its own stratum).
- Drug DILI-concern category: DILIrank (Most, Less, No, Ambiguous), matched by ingredient name.

## 5. Outcome definitions (MedDRA preferred terms as coded in FAERS)
The full term table is built from the list of distinct preferred terms and their TOTAL counts only. It is frozen before any term is tabulated by sex. Rules:
- Hepatocellular (HC): Hepatocellular injury, Hepatic cytolysis, Jaundice hepatocellular.
- Cholestatic (CH): Cholestasis, Hepatitis cholestatic, Cholestatic liver injury, Jaundice cholestatic, Drug-induced cholestasis if present. Excluded: cholestasis of pregnancy, neonatal, familial and benign recurrent forms, cholestatic pruritus.
- Mixed (MX): Mixed liver injury, or a report carrying both an HC and a CH term.
- Unspecified clinical injury (UN): Drug-induced liver injury, Hepatotoxicity, Liver injury, Hepatitis, Hepatitis acute, Hepatitis toxic, Hepatitis fulminant, Hepatic failure, Acute hepatic failure, Subacute hepatic failure, Hepatic necrosis, Jaundice, Coma hepatic, Hepatic encephalopathy, Immune-mediated hepatitis, Autoimmune hepatitis, Liver transplant.
- Primary hepatic case (narrow): any HC, CH, MX or UN term.
- Broad hepatic case (sensitivity): narrow, plus Liver disorder, Hepatic function abnormal, and laboratory terms (transaminase, ALT, AST, hepatic enzyme, liver function test, bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, gamma-glutamyltransferase increases).
- Severe hepatic terms: Hepatic failure, Acute hepatic failure, Subacute hepatic failure, Hepatitis fulminant, Hepatic encephalopathy, Coma hepatic, Hepatic necrosis, Liver transplant.
- Never counted as cases: viral, alcoholic, neoplastic, congenital, infectious, vascular, traumatic and chronic structural liver terms (cirrhosis, fibrosis, steatosis), and procedures other than transplant.
- Overdose context (exclusion flag): any term containing overdose, poisoning, suicid, intentional self-injury, intentional product misuse, drug abuse, substance abuse, or the term Toxicity to various agents.
- Fatal outcome: outcome code DE.
- Alcohol proxy: any reaction or indication term containing alcohol, or the ingredient ALCOHOL or ETHANOL in any role.
- Prior liver disease proxy: an indication term for chronic viral hepatitis, cirrhosis, fatty liver disease, cholangitis, liver transplant or liver neoplasm.

## 6. Covariates
Age band (18 to 49, 50 to 64, 65 or older; 50 is the menopause proxy). Period (2014 to 2017, 2018 to 2021, 2022 onward, by fda_dt). Region (US, non-US, by reporter country then occurrence country). Reporter (health professional: MD, PH, OT, HP, RN; consumer; lawyer; missing). Number of distinct ingredients in any role (1, 2 to 4, 5 to 9, 10 or more). Body weight in kg where recorded.

## 7. Analyses
N1. Hepatic injury reporting by sex within drug.
- Per ingredient with at least 20 narrow hepatic cases in each sex: odds ratio of a hepatic case, women versus men, among reports listing the ingredient as suspect. Woolf standard error. Adult-set estimates adjusted by Mantel-Haenszel over age band, period, region and reporter.
- Pooled: crude sex odds ratio over all reports, and Mantel-Haenszel sex odds ratio stratified by PS ingredient. The difference between the two is the drug-mix component.
- Heterogeneity across ingredients: Cochran Q, I-squared, tau by REML. Each ingredient's deviation from the pooled value is tested with Benjamini-Hochberg control at 5 percent. Empirical-Bayes shrunken estimates are reported.
- Age: the same within women under 50 versus men under 50, and 50 or older, with the sex-by-age interaction per ingredient and pooled.
- Replication: overlap of flagged ingredients with the published VigiBase lists (George et al. 2018), reported as found.

N2. Phenotype by sex.
- Among hepatic cases with an HC, CH or MX phenotype: odds of CH versus HC, women versus men. Crude, then Mantel-Haenszel by PS ingredient, then by PS ingredient and age band.
- Share of the crude log odds ratio removed by stratification, with a bootstrap interval (2000 resamples of reports).
- Validity checks fixed in advance: (i) the cholestatic share must rise with age band, as registries report. (ii) Anchor drugs must show their known signature: clavulanate, flucloxacillin, chlorpromazine, erythromycin toward CH. Isoniazid, diclofenac, valproate, minocycline toward HC. If both checks fail, reporter-coded phenotype is declared uninformative and N2 is reported as a negative result.

N3. Fatal outcome by sex.
- Among narrow hepatic cases: odds of fatal outcome, women versus men. Crude, then Mantel-Haenszel by PS ingredient, age band, period, region and reporter.
- Negative-control calibration: the same stratified sex odds ratio among NON-hepatic reports of the same PS ingredients. The calibrated estimate is the ratio of the two odds ratios (hepatic over non-hepatic). Variances add on the log scale.
- Secondary: severe hepatic term among hepatic cases, by sex, same adjustments.

N4. Sex-stratified correlates (exploratory, adapted from the prediction-model idea).
- Outcome: narrow hepatic case among Adult-set reports. Logistic regression fitted separately in women and men: age band, weight tertile (complete cases, as a secondary model), ingredient count, DILIrank category of the PS ingredient, reporter, region, period, alcohol proxy, prior liver disease proxy.
- Sex interaction for each covariate from a pooled model.
- Reported as correlates of hepatic injury REPORTING. No claim of clinical risk prediction. Discrimination statistics, if shown, are labelled as within-database.

## 8. Sensitivity analyses
Health-professional reports only. US reports only. Broad hepatic definition. Overdose-context reports included. Second-stage deduplication. PS role only versus PS plus SS. Excluding the single largest ingredient stratum.

## 9. Inference rules
Two-sided 95 percent intervals. Benjamini-Hochberg at 5 percent within each family (N1 ingredients, N1 age interactions, N2 ingredients). No causal language. Results are reporting associations. Incidence cannot be estimated from FAERS and is not claimed.

## 10. Reporting
READUS-PV checklist. All scripts, the term table, the ingredient merge table and the file manifest are released with the paper. FAERS files are public.

## Amendments
All four amendments below were made on 2026-11-23 after reading the list of preferred-term NAMES and their TOTAL counts in the first six quarters. No count by sex, by drug or by outcome had been produced.
1. Overdose context is now an explicit list: any term containing "overdose", plus Toxicity to various agents, Completed suicide, Suicide attempt, Intentional self-injury, Poisoning, Poisoning deliberate, Accidental poisoning, Intentional product misuse, Drug abuse, Substance abuse, Drug abuser, Substance abuser, Alcohol poisoning. Reason: the draft rule also matched adverse reactions (Suicidal ideation, Depression suicidal, Suicidal behaviour) and non-drug poisonings (food, metal, carbon monoxide, ergot, chemical).
2. Alcohol proxy excludes terms containing "non-alcoholic". Reason: Non-alcoholic steatohepatitis matched the draft rule.
3. Vanishing bile duct syndrome added to the cholestatic terms. Reason: it is a cholestatic drug-injury phenotype that the draft list omitted.
4. Broad definition only: abnormal-value variants of bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase and gamma-glutamyltransferase added, for consistency with the transaminase terms.
The three amendments below were made on 2026-11-23 after the literature search returned and after the code had been run only on data with the sex column randomly permuted (seed 7). Real sex-specific counts from this dataset had still not been produced. One published table had been read by then: Table 8 of Lu, Suzuki and Wang (2023), which gives counts by sex for 24 drugs in FAERS 2004 to mid-2015.
5. N1 ingredient test: the primary test for each ingredient is a likelihood-ratio test of H0 "the ingredient's sex odds ratio equals the pooled random-effects value", with the Wald test as a check. Reason: Lu, Suzuki and Wang (2023) showed by simulation that ratio-of-ROR subgroup tests inflate the error rate and that a likelihood-ratio test on the within-drug sex by event table does not. The type I error of the test was checked by simulation (0.049 at the 0.05 level).
6. Details the first version left open. N1 age interaction per ingredient requires at least 10 hepatic cases in each of the four sex by age cells. N2 per-ingredient estimates require at least 20 phenotype-coded cases in each sex. N3 non-hepatic comparison reports exclude reports meeting the broad hepatic definition, so the negative control holds no hepatic reports. N1 replication targets the FAERS 2004 to 2015Q2 list of Lu et al. (2023) in addition to the VigiBase lists of George et al. (2018), to the extent those lists can be read.
7. Negative control for the whole pipeline: all analyses are run once with sex permuted. The number of flagged ingredients and interactions under permutation is reported.
8. Made on 2026-11-23 after all 49 quarters were merged and the term table was read once more by NAME and TOTAL count only. Still no tabulation by sex. Later MedDRA versions introduced terms for concepts already on the lists. Added to unspecified clinical injury: Immune-mediated hepatic disorder, Suspected drug-induced liver injury. Added to cholestatic: Immune-mediated cholestasis. Added to overdose context: Suspected suicide, Suspected suicide attempt, Intentional product misuse to child. Radiation overdose removed from overdose context because it is not a drug overdose. Ocular icterus, Bile acids increased and Venoocclusive liver disease were considered and left out, as the first version excludes signs without a diagnosis, unlisted laboratory terms and vascular disease.
The term table was frozen at this point (file terms.csv). The first tabulation by sex followed it.

## Post hoc changes (made AFTER the first real results were seen, 2026-11-23)
These are not pre-specified. The paper labels each of them as post hoc and reports the pre-specified result beside it.
9. Data-quality restriction. The first run showed a cluster of replicated reports with very long suspect lists. Reports naming more than 20 distinct suspect ingredients are 13,168 of 13,710,584 primary-set reports (0.10 percent). They hold 1,893 hepatic cases, of which 1,770 are women, 1,740 come from Canada and 1,132 are fatal. In the pre-specified ingredient scan (primary plus secondary suspects) they produced female shifts of up to 13-fold for every co-listed drug. Action: all main results are also given for the restricted set (at most 20 suspect ingredients). Thresholds of 10 and 5 and exclusion of Canadian reports are sensitivity analyses. The ingredient scan shown in the main text is the pre-specified primary-suspect-only version on the restricted set. The contaminated pre-specified version is reported in the supplement.
10. N2 by age band. Sex odds ratio for cholestatic versus hepatocellular within each age band, stratified by drug. Prompted by the crude shares by age and sex.
11. N3 on the risk-difference scale. Mantel-Haenszel weighted difference in fatal share (women minus men) among hepatic reports and among other reports of the same drugs, and the difference between the two. Prompted by the dependence of an odds-ratio interaction on baseline fatality.
12. Descriptive count of flagged ingredients whose shrunken odds ratio differs from the pooled value by a factor of 1.5 or more.
The changes below were made on 2026-11-24 in answer to an internal hostile review of the claims. All are post hoc.
13. N3 on the absolute scale now uses a common standard. The difference in differences of fatal share (women minus men, hepatic minus other reports) is standardized once to the drug distribution of hepatic reports and once to that of other reports. Change 11 compared two Mantel-Haenszel risk differences with different weights, which is not a like-for-like contrast. It is kept as a third figure. Other reports are described as a within-drug reporting baseline, not a negative control, because the death code records death from any cause.
14. N2 decomposition by direct standardization. The cholestatic share of women and of men is standardized to the combined drug distribution (and drug by age band in adults) over strata that contain both sexes. The share of the crude difference removed is reported with a bootstrap interval. The Mantel-Haenszel log odds ratio share and the Mantel-Haenszel risk difference share are reported beside it.
15. N1 subgroup table. The fully stratified odds ratio is given separately by region, reporter type and period.
16. The mixed-phenotype contrast was not in the plan and is labelled post hoc. Anchor drugs are also tabulated on primary-suspect reports only. The comparison with Lu et al. reports binomial probabilities against base rates. Source fields of the reports with more than 20 suspect ingredients (manufacturer sender, control number, literature reference, report type) were read from the raw files.
Disclosure. The plan, its amendments and all post hoc changes were written on 2026-11-23 and 2026-11-24 and are kept in a private project store with version history. The plan was not registered publicly.
